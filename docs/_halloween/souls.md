# Souls

Soul shape is determined by neuroanatomy. Rules in the afterlife apply differently depending on how close the soul is to a “person-shaped” image.

Humans and catgirls are generally the same family with different traits. Robots are different. Ryko is different again (leftover, halfway between AI, vengeful spirit, and human).

## Humans and catgirls

Neuroanatomy determines the shape. Generally the same family.

Shape: coral-like forms. Triangles facing up with things sticking through them.

Broad description used in notes:
- Upward-facing coral triangle.
- Semi-translucent, faintly bioluminescent coral with denser neural-texture filaments.
- Secondary structures (spines, loops, thin branches) poke through the faces at irregular angles.
- Catgirl variants keep the same overall geometry but shift secondaries: more fluid/curved spines, softer branching, occasional ear- or tail-like filaments that move independently.

Rules: watered-down living physics. Gravity is weak but present. Collision is mostly solid. Sound travels. Personal Babel rooms are relatively stable when they appear. Still alien, but navigable. They can run, climb, and treat doors as doors.

## Robots

Neuroanatomy is an inverted coral triangle, with multiple “points” subtly shifting around.

Broad description used in notes:
- Point-down coral triangle, more crystalline and angular.
- Cleaner surface, hard edges, internal lattice.
- Multiple secondary points (smaller inverted pyramids or spike clusters) slowly migrate across the faces. Motion is subtle, almost tidal. Silhouette never fully settles.

Rules: the void reads them as constructed. Gravity snaps on and off in discrete steps. Collision can fragment the shape into component points that must be reassembled. Babel fragments appear as corrupted logs or schematic overlays. Diagnostic strings can stand in for soul-text. Feels like a hostile debugging environment.

## Ryko

Ryko’s soul is not shaped like a person, so rules apply sideways.

Working visual:
- Sword silhouette.
- Hilt: two interlocking rings of eyes as the base. This part stays oriented.
- Blade: coral-like extrusion, computer-chip texture underneath, human brain texture on top, the two layers sliding against each other and never fully blending.
- The blade goes in and out of the fourth dimension with other shapes. When those shapes would clip, they bitmosh together.
- On-screen text (Japanese, untranslated): 「血液循環は、あなたのような料理人の仕事です。」
  (“Blood circulation is the work of a cook like you.”)
  It never explains itself.

Movement is less walking and more sliding along conceptual edges. Direction can be chosen by pointing the blade; the void then samples the next idea-cluster that way.

Collision is soft and smeary. Phasing through surfaces leaves after-images that can attract afterlife creatures.

Babel fragments for Ryko tend to orbit the hilt rather than form rooms.

Ryko has zero biological components in the sense that matters for plasma exit survival. See `exits-and-plasma.md`.

Nearby person-shaped or robot souls can have their local rules warped slightly by Ryko’s presence (edges of 4D clipping, brief data-smear).

What bothers Ryko specifically (from early notes):
- Loss of body and ordinary agency. Sword form is elegant but wrong for most of what Ryko is good at.
- The Japanese line that never translates and implies a culinary responsibility for blood circulation.
- Babel rooms / shards that contain every judgment or death-recap about the character.
- Being watched / performed for, mirrored by observation-locked creatures.
