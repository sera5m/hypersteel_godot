# Afterlife (non-canon Halloween)

## Premise

When you die, instead of being printed back out immediately, your soul can wander an afterlife. This is a gag and a set-piece, not the default campaign rule.

The afterlife is not a coherent theology. It is constructed of human thought. Every afterlife idea people have ever held gets dumped into the same place, so features merge into sludge.

Examples of merge:
- Christians imagine heaven as clouds, so it gets very foggy.
- Garden / paradise imagery produces patches of greenery.
- Underworld / Sheol / Yomi quietness produces long dim stretches.
- Judgment / scales / courts produce incomplete desks and bridges.
- Liberation / emptiness ideas produce reflective open ground.

Similar concepts stick together. Opposing concepts can also fuse when both are strong. Heaven-garden and hell-fire can become a garden that is also slowly burning. A river can be water and blood at once. Transitions are dreamlike: you notice the fog has become a garden wall without seeing a hard cut.

The place is meant to feel empty but cozy, then wrong when you look closely. Scare comes from conceptual wrongness, patience, and perception rules, not cheap living-world monster slop.

Inspiration is only vague (leftover ideas, axioms, priors, scrambled thought-shapes). Not a straight Gnostic, chaos-magic, or any single-religion implementation.

## Geometry of the place

The afterlife is an incomprehensibly large void with rare structures. You do not travel by reliable physical distance. Structures appear in a random-feeling order because similar (or strongly opposing) ideas stick. Walk far enough and the void re-samples nearby idea-clusters. The same fragment can reappear rotated, scaled, or combined with different debris.

Space is not reliable. Pocket folds and wraps exist. Physics barely has a hold. Gravity drifts. Sound can propagate in odd directions. Doors can lead to the same room rotated in a dimension you cannot see.

A uniform layer of slow-moving, slightly warm blood coats surfaces, about six inches deep in the main void. Footprints fill in after a few seconds. This layer is the most consistent physical rule. It does not fully participate in the dream-logic merges; it just is.

Weather: stormy, low red lightning, dense fog, occasional horizontal rain that is also blood.

Familiar maps can be reskinned and used as rare structures (horror-maze versions), but the default afterlife is the sparse void, not a full map tour.

Miniature Library of Babel rooms / fragments: everything ever written about you, but in incomprehensible text you do not understand. Pages rearrange when you look away. Only scraps of your own deaths or loadout choices may be briefly legible. For Ryko, the same locked Japanese line can appear on fragments.

Gods are not real here, but some parts of god-ideas are vaguely present as leftover shapes.

Intersecting normal reality on the way out can leave plasma. See `exits-and-plasma.md`.

Comedic side idea from early notes: no-clipping with the warp pack in multiplayer can send you to a Backrooms-like sub-layer. Later notes prefer not to lean on infinite-alt-universe slop. Prefer rare edge pockets instead (see `pocket-100m3.md` and cosmology notes there).

## Mood and scare goal

Empty, dark, cold-adjacent, but also cozy in long quiet stretches. Soft fog, low ambient sound, gentle conceptual drift.

Do not slop on cheap monsters. The afterlife has its own bestiary (`bestiary.md`). Living-world roster enemies do not simply appear here reskinned unless a note says so.

Art callouts from early notes (optional, not required):
- Chainsaw Man sliced-astronaut corridor: figures neatly bisected, floating, blood draining against local gravity.
- Blood-coated familiar architecture.
- Bitmoshed static and blood humanoid outlines that watch and dissolve into a second of your own voice.

Objective flavor: wandering until you sort things out. Collecting personal fragments can stabilize a soul enough to leave. Or the plane gets bored and ejects you.
