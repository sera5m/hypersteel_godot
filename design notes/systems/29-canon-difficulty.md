## Suggestions

The “canon” difficulty as a deliberate anti-dissonance mode works.

The core problem is real: the human on the keyboard cannot perceive or act at the speed and precision the chassis is supposed to have. Making the *world* harder (instead of secretly nerfing Ryko) keeps her on-screen capabilities intact while forcing the player to operate closer to the edge of what the fiction claims she can do. Ultrakill’s Must Die / Brutal approach is the right reference.

**On the “sees things twice as fast” idea for permadeath canon:**  
That is a clean diegetic patch. On that difficulty her perception clock runs at 2×, so the player’s real-time reactions map more closely to her in-universe reaction speed. It does not change her movement or strike speed numbers (still 15 m/s sprint, 55 m/s ceiling, 120 m/s punches). It only makes the information arrive faster, which is exactly the gap the human operator has. Easy to communicate in a loading tooltip or suit-AI line: “Perception overclock engaged. Subjective time compression active.”

**Permadeath canon package:**  
Most of it fits the existing tone—fear/menace, “everything is fixable-sorta,” enemies that are trained soldiers rather than HP sponges. The stand-outs that reinforce the fiction without just adding numbers:

- Correct materials required to repair (no passive regen).
- Enemies suicide-bomb, deny healing by self-termination, or call real reinforcements / danger-close / building drops.
- Netrunners, camera vision, Geiger hints, EMP that stops healing, infectious nanobots.
- Bosses that actually learn from the player across attempts (neural net or logged behavior).
- Headshots crack lenses / deafen; reactor puncture from heavy cannon leaves her at 1 HP and dying.
- 8-minute real-time lockout between deaths (or a shorter diegetic “reprint queue” if you want it less punitive).

The reinforcement budget, more spec-ops, drone spam, and “they will drop the building” are all consistent with the campaign’s “unnamed trained soldiers with fear/menace” and the larger-scale spikes.

**Non-permadeath version:**  
Up to 16 satellite-dropped copies, Helldivers-style, while you dodge artillery and intercept missiles on the way down. That keeps the same enemy upgrades but gives the player a reserve of bodies. It also gives a natural story hook (“reprint capacity is finite and contested”) without making every death a full run reset.

Both versions can share the enemy-side upgrades (faster, higher fight IQ, more grenades, wall-pierce, suicide options, learning bosses, etc.). The permadeath branch adds the material-repair restriction, the perception overclock, the longer retry lockout, and the harsher reactor/limb failure states. The non-permadeath branch adds the drop-pod reserve and the mid-air defense sequence.

This stays inside the “real physics + on-screen feats canon” frame. Nothing here requires Ryko to suddenly be town-level; it just makes the existing building-level chassis fight a world that is allowed to use all the tools the setting already has.
